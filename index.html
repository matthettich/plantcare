Quick reference

Signs to watch for

  • Stretching or pale new growth: it needs more light
  • Red or purple blush at the tips: mild light stress, usually fine
  • Pale, washed-out patches after 1–2 weeks: bleaching, so back off the light
  • Wrinkled or soft leaves: it's thirsty
  • Mushy, translucent or black spots: too much water or rot

Dogs 🐕

Keep these up high: all Euphorbias (corn cobs, spiralis, Green Coral, enopla, stenoclada, African milk tree, Coral cactus, and especially Firesticks) and the Millot kalanchoe. Up high is also best for the aloes, jades, Blue chalksticks and Inchworm, which are mildly toxic and usually cause vomiting or drooling. The cacti aren't poisonous, but their spines and glochids hurt mouths and paws. Safe at floor level: the Echeverias, Lola, Pachyveria, Neon Breaker, Coppertone, Fairy washboard, Mistletoe cactus and Lifesaver. If a dog chews something, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control line, (888) 426-4435.

Cold damage

Mild cold stress: red, purple or bronze tint, or slight shriveling, which is harmless. Cold damage: glassy, translucent or water-soaked spots, usually on the side facing the window or at the tips and new growth. These go soft and mushy, then brown or black, or later dry into tan, corky scars. Check by pressing gently: firm is fine, soft means damage. If you see damage, move the plant somewhere warmer, keep it dry and wait a few days to see how far it spreads. Then cut out any mushy tissue back to firm, clean flesh and let it dry. A cheap min/max thermometer on the sill shows how cold it really gets at night.

Lights

Run the lights 12–14 hours a day on a timer. Ease new plants in by starting them 12–14in from the bars, or running the lights 8–10 hours a day, for 1–2 weeks.

Soil

Mix pumice into bagged succulent mix: about 50% for plants with high rot risk, about a third for medium risk. Plants with low risk are fine in plain mix.

New arrivals

Unbox right away and check for cold damage. Don't water on arrival. Repot into dry soil, wait about a week, then water on the plant's next block night.

Root rot

Cut back to firm tissue, let the cuts dry for a few days, repot into dry gritty mix, and wait 1–2 weeks before a light watering. Give it medium light until it re-roots.
